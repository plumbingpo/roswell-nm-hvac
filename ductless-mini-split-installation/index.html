Think about the spaces your central system has never handled well: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs hot all summer, or an older Roswell home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the jobs a ductless mini split was made for. Nothing travels through leaky ductwork, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a three-inch hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor varies its speed to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it runs as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Design is where mini split projects are won or lost, and Roswell HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because an oversized head short-cycles, dehumidifies poorly, and hunts. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched carefully across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk every room with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, in finished line-hide outdoors.

Installation gets the same rigor as our largest systems. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, and flare connections are torqued and leak-checked, since flare joints are where careless installs leak. Condensate is drained or pumped reliably, and the charge is adjusted for line set length per the manufacturer's tables. We commission every zone in both heating and cooling, then hand you the remotes with the settings actually explained. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Most failed mini splits we see in Roswell come from installs that treated the job as simple. Refrigerant leaks at the flares are the headline problem: a joint that was poorly formed or never torqued to spec leaks slowly, and the homeowner finds out a season later when the head blows warm. Moisture is the next issue, because an install that skips the nitrogen pressure test and a micron-verified vacuum leaves water vapor inside a system whose inverter compressor is sensitive to it. Overlong or overly tall line set runs sap capacity quietly, and a drain line without enough fall will drip down the wall as soon as humidity climbs.

Here is the part most installers underestimate: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Roswell HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and testing each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a weekend project. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of a rushed install that never quite works.

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
  • Clean the washable filter screens in each head about once a month, since they clog faster than furnace filters
  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
