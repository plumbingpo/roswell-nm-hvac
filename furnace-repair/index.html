A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In Roswell, NM, the failures cluster at predictable points: a flame sensor coated in oxide that can no longer prove the flame, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

Rather than guess, Roswell HVAC tests the sequence stage by stage. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: the heat exchanger gets inspected, gas connections get leak-tested, and draft gets verified. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so the fire and the airflow are balanced as the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we work on them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are already stocked on the truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower sends through your home. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Replacing the switch when the real issue is airflow removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

Our techs look at every symptom in the context of the whole furnace. We measure flame signal instead of assuming it, which keeps us from replacing a healthy sensor while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve goes unnoticed. Draft is confirmed at the pressure switch and at the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, so airflow issues are caught before they cut years off the heat exchanger. The gas side is leak-tested with instruments, never with a quick sniff. What you get is a furnace proven safe over a full cycle, plus an honest opinion if the unit is getting close to the point where replacing it beats repairing it again.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Change the filter monthly during heating season; it is the cheapest furnace part you will ever buy
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
