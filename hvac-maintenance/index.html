Warning Signs for HVAC Maintenance

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems. Keep an eye out for the following clues:

  • A surprise breakdown during the last hot or cold spell
  • Energy bills creeping up without any change in your habits
  • A newer system whose warranty terms expect documented maintenance
  • Filters you cannot remember changing, or a musty smell when the blower starts
  • You simply have no idea what condition your equipment is actually in
  • Longer run times to get to the thermostat setting
  • Nobody has opened up the system in over a year
  • New noises when the equipment starts up

Our Approach to HVAC Maintenance

The catch is that maintenance only works when it involves measurement, and Roswell HVAC does not do drive-by tune-ups. Our spring cooling visit meters capacitors in microfarads against their ratings, compares compressor and fan amp draws against the data plate, evaluates refrigerant charge by superheat and subcooling, washes the condenser coil, flushes the condensate drain and tests the float switch, and measures the temperature split across the coil. Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Maintenance?

There is a short list of things homeowners can handle: changing filters on time, keeping the outdoor coil clear, leaving registers open, and glancing at the drain. The rest takes instruments, because the problems that leave you stranded look perfectly normal. A capacitor that has lost 20 percent of its rating looks just like a new one. Low charge shows up only in superheat and subcooling readings. Cracked heat exchangers, weak flame signals, marginal pressure switches, and amp draws creeping toward the breaker's limit are all invisible until measured, and all of them cost less to fix before they fail.

Once gas is involved, maintenance becomes a safety issue. A failing burner, flame proving circuit, vent, or draft problem can put combustion products where they do not belong, which is why those items get instrumented verification every year. Roswell HVAC makes those safety checks the core of each fall visit, never an upsell. The records add up, too: because each reading is compared against your history, the trend lines show which components are nearing the end and which have plenty of life, so you replace the drifting part on your own schedule at a planned price, not an emergency one.

Prevention

Maintenance Tips for HVAC Maintenance

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Change the filter every one to three months; it is the single highest-value habit there is
  • Check carbon monoxide alarms monthly in any home with fuel-burning appliances
  • Leave every supply and return register open, including those in unused rooms
  • Get your tune-ups on the calendar during mild weather, well before the rush
  • Give the condenser room to breathe and rinse its coil lightly each spring
  • Clear the condensate drain early in cooling season, before algae has a chance to grow
  • Jot down any unusual sounds or run times, since they help your technician diagnose early
